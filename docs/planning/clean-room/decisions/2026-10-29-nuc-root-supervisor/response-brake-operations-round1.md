# Brake caller amendment: operations response, round 1

I accept the moderator's proposed B: add a separately named `OperatorBrakePolicy` for the existing bo UID and an independently issued brake credential, and preserve `BrakePolicy` and its ordinary-operator exclusion unchanged. This is a consciously different production policy, not evidence that the old policy's distinct-caller condition is met. My [position](position-brake-operations.md) changed from my initial preference for A after considering the actual bo trust boundary and the additional privileged setuid surface. This is consensus to implement and test a scoped design, not production acceptance.

I maintain the [security review's](position-brake-security.md) hard distinction between theft of an approval bearer and compromise of the entire bo account. An ordinary operator token by itself must not operate the brake or reveal its bearer, while a fully compromised bo already holds Docker-root power and is explicitly outside O4's same-UID-compromise claim. We must never describe separate bo-readable files as MFA, human presence or protection against full account compromise. The isolated worker, evaluator, journal, control and model must not inherit bo, its credentials, or Docker access. Kernel peer credentials establish a caller UID and do not prove a human is present.

I also maintain the [recovery review's](position-brake-recovery.md) distinction among admission closure, freezing, killing, an observed empty cgroup, and reconciliation of potentially delayed daemon work. A single successful response or empty observation does not prove all of those events. The stop backend must be able to freeze and kill without waiting on an in-flight launch lock, use its own fixed catalogue for target resolution, and keep unknown outcomes closed until ownership and daemon operations are reconciled. A process or daemon timeout alone never licenses an automatic restart or retry.

The proposed production frontend must require both the configured bo peer and the independently issued stop bearer for stop and status. The frontend's separate UID and root backend's authenticated peer must be verified through actual Unix sockets, with no caller-selected PID, path, command, unit, or Docker options. The key must stay out of argv, environment, logs, model requests, generated artifacts and worker mounts. Wrong UID, ordinary approval bearer, wrong stop key, malformed operation, and unknown target must be denied; verify that workers and other service roles cannot use the channel even when handed the ordinary token. This preserves the stronger existing policy for its existing consumers rather than modifying its constructor or pretending bo is not an ordinary operator.

Acceptance still requires the real fixed operator command and exact deployed frontend/backend, with separate-key custody reviewed and outage tests against owned targets: ordinary control, database, journal, gateway and Docker-client unavailability; held launch lock; restart or lost reply; and stop verification through the kernel. Where a dependency is simulated unavailable, say so and do not upgrade the observation into host-loss or daemon-crash recovery. Do not promote the evaluated candidate or claim production INT-LOOP from this design decision. If these tests expose unacceptable credential access, missing target identity, or a dependence on the ordinary control plane, withhold deployment and reassess rather than falling back silently to a weaker policy.
