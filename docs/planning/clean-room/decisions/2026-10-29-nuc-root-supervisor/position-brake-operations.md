# Brake caller amendment: operations position

I recommend **B: an explicitly named production policy for bo's kernel UID plus a separately held brake bearer**, while leaving the existing `BrakePolicy` and its operator-exclusion invariant intact. This is a proposed amendment, not a claim that the current frontend satisfies the old policy. The actual deployed brake must be exercised through the new policy before acceptance.

I initially favored A because its additional caller UID preserves the existing code's documented distinction. The security and recovery positions expose the operational cost: a root-owned setuid executable adds privilege transitions and another installation and recovery surface, yet any process controlling bo could still invoke it. Since bo already has Docker-root authority, it cannot provide the stated stronger account-compromise separation. The actual O4 decision requires a separate human operator account and action-bound approval, without an MFA or same-UID-compromise claim; R9 separately requires an out-of-band brake credential and independence from the ordinary operator token. Those requirements should be checked directly, not replaced with an artificial peer distinction. See [O4 and R9](../../consensus-plan.md), [security position](position-brake-security.md), and [recovery position](position-brake-recovery.md).

B is acceptable only if the factory's worker, evaluator, control, journal and model execution cannot use bo's account or read the brake credential, and if the independently issued bearer is absent from prompts, worker mounts, arguments, environment and logs. A stolen ordinary approval bearer must not authorize stop or disclose the brake key. The permitted peer and the independent secret both need checking at the brake frontend. A separate UID 2003 serves that frontend, and its kernel-authenticated connection to the root stop backend must remain the only root control path. A full compromise of bo is outside this boundary and two files readable by bo are not two factors. `SO_PEERCRED` verifies a process credential, not a human or physical presence. [Unix sockets](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/unix.7.html).

Operationally, keep a fixed command and opaque generation identifier; the root backend resolves the target through its own immutable catalogue. The frontend must not receive arbitrary PID, path, shell, systemd unit, Docker flags or executable. Requests and responses need bounds and generic refusals for unauthorized peers and malformed or unknown targets. A successful response needs separate evidence of admission closure, cgroup freeze and observed emptiness, with any unresolved Docker operation explicitly retained. An empty cgroup at one instant does not prove that a delayed engine start cannot later occur. The root-owned freeze/closure mechanism must be independent of any lock held by the ordinary launcher and remain useful when the control service, journal, PostgreSQL, gateway and Docker client are unavailable. See [root-supervisor consensus](consensus-plan.md) and [cgroup v2](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html).

A remains a viable fallback if a distinct caller UID is confirmed as a non-negotiable requirement. It needs a reviewed locked UID and a minimal native setuid client with real-UID checks, no input-selected executable, explicit inherited-FD and environment handling, supplementary-group clearing, permanent saved/effective/real UID and GID drop, and no retained capabilities. The transition must fail closed under `nosuid`, `no_new_privs`, tracing, failed group removal and other failures; Linux documents that supplementary groups survive exec and `setuid` alone does not clear them. These obligations add real privileged code and tests, while failing to resist full control of bo. [Linux credentials](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/credentials.7.html), [setgroups](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/setgroups.2.html), [execve](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/execve.2.html).

C is not ready for selection. A polkit/systemd mechanism could present a distinct principal without setuid, but the systemd manager's unit-management action encompasses several operations; a usable production choice must demonstrate fixed method, exact unit and verb, noninteractive headless behavior, no transient arbitrary unit or property authority, and correct behavior with the system bus unavailable. A narrow privileged broker may satisfy these constraints, but adds a separate availability and recovery dependency. [systemd manager API](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/257/org.freedesktop.systemd1.html), [polkit architecture](https://polkit.pages.freedesktop.org/polkit/polkit.8.html).

Before adopting B, prove with actual host peers and a separately provisioned stop secret that the right bo caller and bearer stop exactly the intended generation; wrong peer, approval bearer, wrong brake key, unknown target and malformed requests do not. Test that worker/control/evaluator/journal cannot successfully operate the brake, including with an exposed ordinary token; verify credential path ownership and permissions, and do not put the key in any generated artifact. Exercise busy launch, unavailable disposable dependencies, frontend/backend loss and restart using owned resources. Demonstrate the operator command is usable without sudo, Docker, an extra SSH key or the ordinary operator token. Keep unknown launch outcomes frozen until reconciled.

My hard positions are: do not relabel the existing `BrakePolicy` as satisfying B; do not silently exclude bo from its ordinary identity list; do not assert MFA or same-account compromise resistance; and do not call the brake production-ready until the actual new frontend, independent bearer, fixed root backend, availability and recovery behavior pass. This recommendation selects a smaller proposed policy change under the documented threat model; it does not authorize deployment or promotion by itself.
