# Security concession response

**Concede the moderator's operator scope.** Existing `bo` UID 1000 can serve as the local operator if the deployed control, worker, evaluator, journal and brake frontend use distinct identities and actual socket peers are checked. The user-selected O4 mechanism is a separate local operator account and action-bound approval channel; its recorded scope expressly makes **no MFA or resistance-to-same-UID-compromise claim**. [O4 selection](../2026-10-26-operator-auth/consensus-plan.md#post-debate-human-selection-2026-10-26) I withdraw any implication that a new human SSH key, new account for `bo`, or resistance to a compromised `bo` process is required for this MVP. Existing `bo` has Docker-group root-equivalent authority and is trusted as an administrator. That trust limit must be visible; no change to its groups, login or sudo is implied.

**Maintain the independent stop boundary, with the clarified scope.** A separately supervised nonroot brake frontend should have a dedicated identity and a separate stop bearer/custody from ordinary action approval; its root backend may resolve only fixed stop targets. The backend must verify the frontend's actual peer and authorized stop operation rather than trusting a caller-supplied UID. A stolen approval bearer alone must have no brake authority, and an operator cannot turn the stop API into a generic privileged process controller. Two credentials readable by `bo` are not MFA and do not resist compromise of `bo`; the earlier paper's stronger custodian model is a possible future assurance, not an extra MVP blocker under O4. Test stop without control, database, gateway or Docker, and separately identify what happens when the frontend or backend is unavailable. [Moderator synthesis](moderator-round1.md)

**Maintain lifecycle closure and accept the scoped Moby reconciliation.** The helper records one-use launch intent and durable closure in its protected catalogue. Stop must report closure, freeze, kill, empty observation and engine reconciliation as distinct states. Unknown starts remain closed and frozen; a client timeout or an empty subtree does not cancel a daemon operation. For the pinned daemon, successful non-force removal of an exact owned full ID followed by 404 and same-inode frozen/empty checks is a source-backed, conditional reconciliation criterion, not a general proof of host or daemon recovery. The observed positive-marker and frozen populated child-scope canary supports implementation only. [Canary and source audit](../../verification/2026-10-29-nuc-late-start-freeze-canary.md) Owner-loss, withheld start acknowledgment and worker migration denial remain pre-admission tests.

**Concede staged installation without activation.** An exact reviewed plan may provision narrowly named identities, protected directories and units while keeping services and runtime admission disabled, subject to collision checks and preserved existing records. That permission does not validate its installed paths, private journal connectivity, root socket authentication or first task. One fixed greenfield catalogue recipe is an appropriately bounded first integration, not a multi-task factory or a completed INT-LOOP. Actual private positive/negative canaries, pinned inputs and the scoped resource boundaries must precede admission; budget rules outside this task retain their existing scope.

**Maintain honest recovery acceptance.** Task-owned fault injection and isolated equivalents can establish the behavior they exercise without disrupting shared Docker, systemd or host services. A simulated unavailable socket is not an observed daemon crash, helper SIGKILL is not system-manager loss, and fsync calls do not prove physical power-loss durability. Keep specific gates open whenever the necessary safe test is unavailable; do not turn implementation permission into production acceptance or promotion approval.

There is **no remaining HIGH design disagreement** with the five proposed resolutions. HIGH implementation and evidence gaps remain: authenticated stop independent of Docker and the main services; closure across owner loss and an accepted or lost start; cgroup migration denial; protected catalogue and peer identity provisioning; and integration with the authoritative task/lease/journal and action-bound promotion paths. These are acceptance gates, not reasons to invent a new human decision or to claim the present fixture is deployed.
