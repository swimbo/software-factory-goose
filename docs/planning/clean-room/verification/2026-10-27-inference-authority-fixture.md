# Unregistered inference reservation and dispatch fence

The experimental `InferenceAuthority` owns a fresh Restate object namespace, with an integer 750,000-microUSD unit cap and 256 retained call records. An exclusive `prepare` binds one canonical call ID to the request digest, review digest, model, attempt and reservation amount in the same ledger state. Exact retries preserve the current stage and do not reserve again; changed bindings conflict. Denied calls cannot begin, and capacity exhaustion never evicts history.

An exclusive `begin` changes a reserved call to `DispatchingUnknown` before returning. The stage cannot be reset; subsequent begin attempts reject, including after an acknowledgement is lost. This intentionally treats even a call that may not actually have reached a provider as unknown. No provider call is made by this object, and a successful begin reply is not a self-enforcing single-use capability: a future trusted sole launcher must be bound to this authority and must not issue multiple calls using one reply.

After an initial missing-implementation compilation failure, three focused tests and strict all-feature/all-target control-plane Clippy passed. The rebuilt disposable Restate/PostgreSQL replay fixture passed twice. It verified exact prepare retry; changed request, review, model, attempt and amount conflicts; retained budget denial; a discarded begin acknowledgement followed by refusal and `DispatchingUnknown` status; and ten simultaneous begin requests with exactly one success. A reserved call remained reserved after graceful and SIGKILL restarts and could then begin once; a previously begun call remained unknown and could not begin again. The SIGKILL followed acknowledged state changes; no in-flight Restate mutation or actual provider dispatch was crashed.

The prototype is registered only in the disposable fixture. It does not update the current `GatewayClient`, old `BudgetCounter`, or production routes. Its request fields are caller-supplied until a trusted authenticated authority derives and persists them from an actual lease and canonical request. A separate namespace cannot safely coexist as another spending authority for an existing unit; exclusive ledger migration or retirement, daily cap, price review, provider-side idempotency, streaming, outcome reconciliation and trusted launcher containment remain open. Synthetic hashes and IDs are binding examples, not evidence of a real provider request.
