# Gateway helper in a user namespace

A network-disabled Linux x86-64 fixture replayed the pinned `linux_gateway_exec.py` in a newly created user namespace, with synthetic same-number Root, gateway and broker identity mappings. The actual helper checked its inherited socket and executable, installed its transfer filter, waited at its gate, rechecked the socket and cgroup, and executed the existing synthetic Go target. The target's filter and descendant probes passed; an unconfined, capability-free same-host-UID sibling was denied both ptrace and `pidfd_getfd` on the actual listening socket after exec. A separate unconfined same-UID positive control succeeded. The synthetic broker observed the target's expected PID and UID.

Negative cases refused a non-stream socket and a closed gate. After helper readiness, replacing the socket pathname with a different correctly owned socket caused the final check to refuse execution. The fixture ran with no-new-privileges, disabled outer seccomp and AppArmor profiles to avoid masking the attack, and used the host's existing Yama scope 0. Its user namespaces and sockets were disposable; no shared policy changed.

This validates compatibility of the helper and its filter with **one synthetic mapping**, not production admission. The fixture controller directly constructs mappings and does not use the actual Root launcher, guard, wallet, Bifrost route, credentials or production configuration. It does not prove map authority, egress fencing, external descriptor custody, cgroup migration, supervisor/daemon/host loss, or descendant cleanup. The mapped-root and broker-group implications still require review. The $5/day and $0.75/task caps remain binding, the candidate is disabled and live attempts remain **0/120**.
