# Namespaced gateway guard-loss rehearsals

Three network-disabled x86-64 rehearsals moved a gated gateway barrier, created with the pinned user-namespace bootstrap, into a real attempt-cgroup leaf. They then exercised an actual PID1 guard against supervisor-group loss, guard-main crash and current-lease expiry. In each case the guard fenced the synthetic gateway and worker, the owner revocation was acknowledged, and the owned attempt slice was subsequently confirmed absent. The guard reached inactive/success after owner loss and lease expiry; the deliberately crashed guard reached failed/signal after its stop-post cleanup. Exact identities and source hashes are in the [receipt](2026-11-02-userns-guard-loss.json).

The first lease-expiry replay fenced the processes but its stop-post later failed after the fixture retired its slice and synthetic owner before PID1 finished. The exact stop-post error was not captured. That guard remains recorded as failed and its exact slice is absent. The fixture now waits for the guard's terminal state before retiring those resources; a fresh lease-expiry replay passed with the guard inactive/success. The earlier result is retained in the receipt, not counted as successful cleanup.

These are component rehearsals. The actual Root constructor, worker prepare/start and PID1 guard ran, but the gateway barrier was owned by a separate fixture controller, not the private Root gateway launcher. The catalog, registry and owner were synthetic, the socket lived in private temporary storage, and no production Bifrost route or credentials were used. They do not establish handed-socket cleanup, full namespace lifetime custody, provider charge bounds, daemon or host loss, or production admission. The candidate remains disabled, the $5/day and $0.75/task caps remain binding, and no paid benchmark attempts ran.
